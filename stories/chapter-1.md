# Chapter 1

---

Luna got into her bed again.

This was not a surprise. Luna had done this enough times that Aoi had stopped asking why, partly because the reasons were always different and equally implausible, and partly because she'd realised at some point that asking gave Luna an audience, and Luna with an audience was worse than Luna just being silently in her bed. So: here they were. Aoi on her back, Luna somewhere slightly to her left and probably sprawled, because Luna slept like a person who had never considered that other people existed in space.

Aoi stared at the ceiling.

She wasn't going to sleep. She could feel that in the way you can feel it — the particular flatness behind the eyes that's too tired to be alert and too wired to drop off. She'd felt it all day, this weight that wasn't heavy so much as just... constant. Like static. The kind of tired that work makes you, not the kind that sleep fixes.

The kind, she did not let herself think further about.

Luna shifted and ended up closer. Aoi didn't move away.

This was new, technically. She would normally have moved. She would normally have said something — sleep on your own side, I'm serious, you're like a furnace — and there would have been the back-and-forth of it, Luna being insufferable, Aoi drawing a line, and then some version of the same standoff they'd been having since October. She didn't do any of that. She just lay there with Luna's warmth at her side and kept staring at the ceiling, and the ceiling had nothing to offer.

---

The thing about her family was that people didn't get it unless they'd lived it, and she'd long since stopped explaining. The shorthand version — parents, brother, the usual story — didn't carry any of the actual texture of it, the way it felt to grow up understanding you were the wrong shape for the space you'd been given. Her brother had fit that space perfectly and had never, as far as she could tell, thought about it for a single day in his life. Her parents had softened eventually, as people do when time does the work that communication couldn't, but by then she'd already put the distance there herself and found she didn't know how to close it. Spite had calcified into habit. Habit had calcified into just — how things were.

She wasn't thinking about that specifically. She was not, in fact, thinking about much. That was the thing about this kind of tired, the variant that work had been giving her lately; it emptied you out and then left you there, hollow, unable even to properly ruminate. She would have preferred to ruminate. Rumination at least had momentum.

Luna breathed next to her. Slow. Already under.

Aoi found, without quite meaning to, that she'd turned slightly, that one arm had ended up loosely around Luna, that her face was angled toward the window rather than the ceiling. She could see the edge of the city through the gap in the curtain, the orange-grey that passed for dark in a place where nothing was ever fully dark. Some building's light. Some street's particular glow.

She didn't know when she'd started holding on. She hadn't decided to.

---

Luna woke up, and Aoi was still there.

This was, in the particular logic of how things usually went between them, not the expected outcome. The expected outcome was Aoi coming to consciousness at some slightly unreasonable hour and immediately registering Luna as a problem to be solved. Complaint. Negotiation. Luna getting the shoulder. The routine of it.

Instead: Aoi, awake, very still, arms around her, staring at the window. Not asleep. Not going back to sleep. Just — there, in the dark, with that look on her face that Luna didn't have a name for because she had never seen it on Aoi before.

She didn't move. Didn't say the usual thing. Aoi hadn't moved either — hadn't registered that Luna was awake, or had registered it and didn't react, which was somehow worse.

"Aoi."

A beat.

"Hm?"

Like everything was fine. Like she'd just been asked if she'd eaten. That specific flatness in a voice that normally had bite to it, or impatience, or at minimum the particular energy of someone who found Luna's existence moderately exhausting. There was none of that. There was nothing.

Luna kept very still.

She didn't know what to do here. That was the honest accounting of it. She and Aoi were not — they weren't that. Luna irritated Aoi, Aoi tolerated Luna, somewhere in there was an arrangement that had started as adversarial and had worn smooth at the edges without ever becoming something else, or nothing that had a name. This was not the arrangement calling for anything in particular from her. She was the one who got kicked out of the bed. She was not the one who knew what to do when the kicking stopped.

So she did nothing. Lay there. Listened to the city doing its 2 AM version of itself. Aoi didn't move. The window held whatever Aoi was looking for in it and didn't give it back.

---

The sigh, when it came, was not a normal sigh. It was the kind that had been stored somewhere too long — shaky at the edges, the sound of a person trying to let out a small amount of pressure and discovering there was more behind it than they'd planned for.

"I'm sorry," Aoi said.

Luna waited for the rest of it. There was no rest of it.

She wasn't apologising for anything that had happened tonight. That was clear enough. There was no inflection of specificity to it, no you-in-particular. It was the apology of someone who had gotten tired of carrying themselves around and had addressed it in the only direction available.

Luna did not say it's okay. Did not say you don't need to apologise. She'd thought of both and they were wrong, both of them, the kind of words that exist to give the speaker somewhere to put the discomfort rather than because they're true. So she said nothing. Kept her hand where it was, loosely, not making anything of it. Just the fact of her being there, which she couldn't do much about at this point.

The silence settled back.

Then: a tear. Not dramatic — Aoi wasn't doing anything dramatic, wasn't shaking or heaving. It was just a tear, and then after a while another one, and then a third, in the way water comes through a crack in something that's been holding for too long. Her face was still mostly in control. Her body was not consulting her face.

Luna felt it before she saw it — the slight change in breathing, the way Aoi's grip shifted, tighter, without Aoi seeming to notice she'd done it. She didn't say anything. She pulled Aoi closer in a way that tried not to be a statement about itself, just a small adjustment, just arrangement of space. Started moving her hand in slow circles on Aoi's back. Not comfort exactly — she didn't have the language for that, never had. Just something to do with the hand. Just evidence.

It took a long time to become crying properly. The process was grinding and ugly, not the clean release of a good cry but the kind where the body is working against itself, where numbness fights the breaking down even as it's happening. Aoi went through it without speech, without any of the words people usually fill crying with. Luna rubbed her back and kept her mouth shut.

The wet came through the fabric. That was when she knew it had been going on for a while.

She thought about what she knew about Aoi's life, in the way you know things about a person you've been in proximity to — not because they told you, exactly, but because being in someone's space means you absorb it through the walls, through the phone calls you're not meant to hear, through the way she moved after a shift at work versus the way she moved any other time. The family situation. The brother. The work that was doing something to her, gradually, in the way certain jobs did — not a single wound but erosion. Nothing in her life sitting right, and instead of saying that to anyone she'd just kept shedding the people who might have noticed.

Luna had stayed. Not for a reason she could make sound good. Aoi had said things, across the months, that landed worse than she'd probably meant them to, or maybe exactly as she'd meant them to, and Luna had stayed anyway, not as an act of virtue but just because — Aoi was there. Aoi was *there*, every day, difficult and tired and occasionally horrible and real in a way that made the apartment feel like a place someone actually lived rather than a space Luna was between things in. That was the whole of it. No poetry.

---

"Why do you tolerate me."

She'd known it was coming. Or something like it. The question when you've been crying at someone for the better part of an hour and you can't explain why and you need some shape put to the incomprehensibility of another person choosing to stay in the room.

Luna didn't answer immediately.

The answer, she knew, was not going to be satisfying. There was no version of it that would land as comfort, nothing she could say that would sound like enough reason. That was just the truth. You didn't have good reasons for the things that turned out to matter; you had the thing itself, the accumulated fact of it, and that was all.

"You were just — there," she said. "When you didn't have to be either."

Aoi didn't say anything. Luna pulled the blanket over both of them — the room was cold, the heating in the building being the sort of suggestion rather than a system — and that was that. No resolution to it. Just the answer, sitting there, inadequate and honest.

---

Aoi's breathing changed at some point, the ragged quality of it softening as the crying ran out of something to run on. Not peace — she wasn't peaceful, she wouldn't be peaceful for a while — but the specific exhaustion of a person who had used everything up and had nothing left to hold the rest of it with. She tucked herself in tighter, face under Luna's chin.

Luna kissed the top of her head without thinking. It was out before she'd decided to do it, too small to call a decision. She left her mouth there a second and then didn't, just her chin against Aoi's hair, both of them still.

Aoi didn't say anything about it. Maybe she hadn't registered it. Maybe she had and it was the wrong moment to register it, or the right moment, or some other category that didn't have a name.

She went under slowly. Not a soft sleep but a falling one, the particular graceless drop of someone too tired to manage the transition. Luna held on. The city did its thing outside — quieter now, or it felt quieter, sound and space both settling into whatever 3 AM was. She stayed awake for a while after. Not for any reason. Just — awake.

---

The sound that woke them later wasn't anything. A door, maybe. Nothing.

They were close. Faces close, in the way sleep arranges things without permission. Warm. The hour had gone soft around them, 2:50 something, and Luna was half-under still and Aoi was half-there, and the dark had that particular quality where the usual metrics didn't apply and things were mostly just proximity and breathing.

The kiss was not a decision that got made. It was the outcome of two people being too close together at too strange an hour, of something that had been in the room already, for longer than either of them had let themselves notice. There was the brushing of it, accidental or not-quite-accidental, and then there was the moment of staying, of not pulling back, of it becoming itself — brief and quiet and real, not tentative, not performed.

Then it was over.

Neither of them moved. The city. The dark. Whatever had just been confirmed between them sat in the room without commentary, because there was nothing to say at 3 AM that wouldn't make it smaller than it was.

They went back to sleep.

---

Morning was grey and thin and mostly uneventful.

They woke tangled — that was the word for it, no other word fit — legs overlapped, Aoi's hand somewhere near Luna's ribs, faces too close, the full inconvenient inventory of two people who'd slept in the same narrow bed without maintaining any of the usual spatial treaties. The light made everything specific. Aoi surfaced first and lay there for a moment registering all of it, the physical data of the night, and then sat up.

"Sorry," she said. Her voice was the morning version, flat and low. "For being — sorry. All of it."

Luna had her by the wrist before she could get any further.

Not hard. Just — there. A fact.

Aoi looked at her hand and then at Luna, who was horizontal and sleep-creased and giving her the expression of someone who had already decided something and was not going to be argued with.

"Stay," Luna said.

Not a question.

Aoi stayed.
