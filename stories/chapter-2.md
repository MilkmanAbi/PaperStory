# Chapter 2

---

The thing about Luna's job was that it required her to be a person all day.

This was not, technically, a complaint. She liked people, mostly — liked the specific texture of being around them, the way conversations had momentum of their own and took you places you hadn't planned to go. She was good at it, even. People responded to her in ways that surprised her sometimes, given that she wasn't trying to do anything in particular, just being whatever she was that day. But being whatever she was that day, reliably, eight-plus hours at a stretch, turned out to cost something. By the time she was on the train home she'd feel it — not in her legs or her back the way she might from physical work, but in a more general sense of having been running and now slowing, the particular winding-down of a machine that had been doing its job all day.

The train was loud in the way commuter trains always were — collective human noise, muffled conversations, the shudder and sway of it. She stood for most of the ride because it was that hour and there were no seats, one hand around the rail overhead, her bag hanging against her hip. Around her people were on their phones or staring at the middle distance with the particular expressionlessness of people who had decided not to be anywhere until they got somewhere.

She recognised it. She was probably doing it too.

---

The walk from the station was ten minutes, which usually she liked — it was the decompression, the buffer between being out in it and being home. Tonight she just walked it. Noticed the usual things without really looking: the convenience store on the corner still bright, a dog being walked by someone in a coat already, the specific chill that had moved in since last week that meant seasons were shifting.

The apartment was on the third floor, no elevator. She took the stairs the way she always did, which was to say she didn't think about them.

The lights were on inside. Aoi was home.

This was not unusual — Aoi's hours were different, more structured, she was usually back by six. But Luna still registered it, the same way she registered it every time: the apartment's quality changed when someone else was in it. The difference between empty space and inhabited space, which is hard to articulate and completely obvious when you're in it.

She put her bag down and her shoes off and went to the kitchen to figure out if there was anything to eat that wasn't work.

---

She made rice. This was the path of least resistance and also she was hungry in the specific way where complicated food sounded like too many decisions. While it cooked she leaned against the counter and looked at her phone for a while without reading anything on it, just scrolling the way you do when your eyes want somewhere to be.

From the other room she could hear nothing. Not silence exactly — there was always background, the building, the street below, some neighbour's television at low volume — but nothing from Aoi specifically. No typing, no movement, no sounds of the usual end-of-day activity.

She thought about calling through to ask if she'd eaten. Didn't, for no particular reason.

When the rice was done she took her bowl and went through to the main room.

---

Aoi was at her desk. Not working, as far as Luna could tell — the laptop was open but its light was secondary to the window, which Aoi was looking through rather than at anything specific. The trees outside caught the orange of the residential lights on their undersides, dark above and lit below, and beyond them the sky had gone that particular evening navy that wasn't quite dark enough to be night but had given up being dusk. The street below was quieter than it would be in an hour, when people finished eating and walked their dogs and did the things people did at 8 PM. Right now there was just the occasional car, the occasional voice.

Aoi didn't turn around.

Luna sat down on the floor by the couch, which was where she usually sat when she was eating in here, her back to the seat cushions. She ate a bit. The rice was fine. Outside, something flickered — a cyclist going past, the sweep of their light across the low branches.

She wasn't thinking about last night. She was aware of last night the way you're aware of a change in weather — you don't have to think about it for it to have changed how everything feels slightly different. It was there. Neither of them had said anything today, in the morning when they'd managed coffee and the slow process of being upright and human again. Not avoidance so much as — there not being anything yet that fit into words. That was okay. Luna was not in a hurry about it.

She watched Aoi not doing anything.

There was something she'd always noticed about Aoi when Aoi thought she wasn't being watched, which was that she became less defended. The deliberateness went out of her posture. She sat differently — not worse, just more like a person who had briefly stopped maintaining anything. It didn't happen often. Luna had catalogued the specific conditions under which it occurred, not intentionally, just because she paid attention to things.

Right now was one of them. Aoi had her elbow on the desk and her chin on her hand, and she was looking at the trees and whatever was beyond the trees that couldn't be seen from here, and she looked tired in a way that had nothing to do with sleep.

Luna finished her rice.

---

She put the bowl on the floor beside her and stayed where she was, back against the couch. The room was dim — Aoi hadn't turned on the overhead, just the desk lamp, which didn't do much and seemed like a concession to the idea of working rather than actual working. It made the room feel smaller and warmer than it was, and the window more like a painting of outside than outside itself.

She should do things. There were things. Laundry that had been suggestions for three days. Something she'd meant to look up. A message she'd been composing in her head during the commute.

She stayed where she was.

There was a specific kind of quiet that happened when two people were in a room and neither of them needed anything from the other. Luna had lived with enough people — family, flatmates, various transient arrangements — to know it was rarer than it should be. Most shared quiet had something underneath it, some negotiation in progress, some awareness of the other person that required management. This was different. Aoi wasn't performing not-talking. Luna wasn't performing comfortable with the silence. They were just both in the room and both tired and both looking at the same window from different angles.

Luna thought, not for the first time, that she liked this apartment best at this hour. Not because of anything in particular. Just the hour itself, the way the city got quieter without going away, the way the light outside changed from a daytime kind to an evening kind that was less demanding.

---

She thought about Aoi's family the way she sometimes found herself thinking about things she'd absorbed without meaning to — the phone calls she'd half-heard through walls, the particular flatness Aoi got when she'd just gotten off one. She didn't have the full shape of it and she'd never asked, because Aoi was not someone you asked directly unless you wanted the shutters to come down immediately, and also because the details didn't feel like the point. The point was just: Aoi had been carrying something for a long time and had gotten very good at carrying it alone, and was not particularly skilled at knowing what to do when she didn't have to do that.

Luna was not sure what she was, in Aoi's life, that had made last night possible. The proximity of it, probably — just that she'd been there, consistently, in the aggravating way she'd been there, until she wasn't a stranger anymore even if she wasn't anything nameable either. She hadn't done anything in particular to earn it. She'd just stayed in the general area long enough.

This was either a very simple thing or a very complicated one. She hadn't decided which.

---

At some point Aoi turned from the window.

Not toward Luna specifically — she just turned, the way you do when you've been looking at one thing for too long, redirecting without destination. She looked at the room, at the bowl on the floor, at Luna, with the slight adjustment of someone remembering she'd been in here.

"Did you eat?" she said.

"Yeah." Luna indicated the bowl. "Made rice."

Aoi looked at the bowl and then back at the window, or past it. "Okay."

She didn't say anything else. She didn't turn back to the laptop either. Just sat there in the medium dark with her hand still on her chin.

Luna looked at her for a moment, then looked at the window she'd been looking at. The trees were doing what trees did at 7 PM in autumn, which was to say they were just there, losing their outlines to the dark but still present. You could still tell they were trees. The light on their undersides from the streetlamps was orange and specific, and above them the sky was the colour of something not quite started.

"It's nice," Luna said, about nothing in particular.

A pause.

"Yeah," Aoi said.

And that was it. That was the whole of it. Luna stretched her legs out in front of her, crossed them at the ankle, and settled against the couch, and Aoi stayed where she was, and they both looked at the window and whatever was beyond the window, and the city did its evening things around them quietly and without requiring anything in response.

Luna felt the specific weight of tired that her body had been accumulating all day settle into the floor through her, and thought, briefly and without particular drama, that this was fine.

This was enough.
