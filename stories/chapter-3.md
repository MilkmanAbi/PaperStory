# Chapter 3

---

The evening happened, as it did, weirdly calm in its comfortable silence. Luna had been rummaging in the kitchen, looking for whatever to eat, maybe a cold beer for the night - It'd be nice to just have a cold can, enjoy the silence, the day following was a weekend, and she'd wanted to relax. Aoi watched idly, just as numb as the evening sky.

Aoi stared at Luna, she was rummaging like a famished teen searching for snacks again. "What are you even looking for...?", she'd asked, almost tiredly, just as much mentally checked out.

Luna, just looked at Aoi, peeking from the kitchen rack for just a moment, "Hungry... bored.".
"Oh... 'kay..."
"You want somethin' too?"
"I don't even think we have anything worthwhile, though..."
"Eh... Chotto… Meiwaku.", Luna just made this face, she knew they didn't stock up on the finest snacks... Didn't need to be reminded though.
"Don't make that face at me."
"I'm not making a face."
"You were about to."
"I was going to ask if there's anything else then, you're judging me too much. Hmph."
"There's rice."

A beat.

Luna stared blankly. Rice, really? Was dinner just supper now? At least they had furikake, a beer or two in the fridge...

---

So they ate. Simple enough - whatever they could cobble together, tofu, rice, the furikake Luna had grabbed like it was a personal victory. Two cold cans between them on the small table, takeout containers nudged to one side to make room. Luna cracked hers open first. Aoi followed, a moment later, like she'd been waiting to see if Luna would first.

The overhead light was too bright. It always was. Neither of them had done anything about it.

"How was today...," Luna asked, not really asking.

"Fine."

Luna gave her a look.

"That's not an answer, y'know."

"It's a short one."

"...Mm." She drank. "Mine was stupid. Had a meeting. Then another one about the first one."

Aoi just looked at her. "That's most of work."

"Is yours like that?"

"Mine's just—" Aoi paused, like she was trying to find the word for it. "A lot. All at once. Then nothing for twenty minutes. Then a lot again."

"That sounds worse than meetings, honestly."

Aoi shrugged, the smallest one. "It is what it is."

---

They ate for a while. The pot on the stove had gone cold, clicking quietly to itself. Outside, something — a car, a bike, nothing — passed and faded. Luna was looking at the window. Their reflections in it, slightly ghost-like, the smeared orange of the streetlamps beyond the glass.

"Do you ever just... not want to go back," she said.

"To work?"

"In the morning. When the alarm goes off."

Aoi's chopsticks stopped.

"Everyone does."

"I know. I'm asking if you do."

A beat.

"...Yeah."

"What do you do about it."

"Go anyway."

Luna nodded slowly. Just, yeah. Of course. What else was there.

"Yeah..."

---

They finished eating. Or mostly — Aoi had pushed her rice around for a bit before setting her chopsticks down like that was a decision. Luna was on her second half of the can, not rushing it, just letting the cold of it sit in her hand.

"Can I ask you something," she said.

"You're going to regardless."

"Your family."

Aoi put her chopsticks down properly this time.

"...You don't have to," Luna added. "I've just heard the calls. I don't really know what I'm hearing."

A long pause. Aoi picked up her beer. Drank.

"It's not interesting."

"I'm not asking 'cause it's interesting."

Aoi looked at her then, just for a second. Set the can down carefully.

"My brother's better at everything. Or he was, or—" she stopped. "It doesn't matter. My parents wanted something I wasn't. I was angry about it for a while and then I got tired of being angry and then I just... stopped going home as much. And they let me."

"They let you."

"It was easier. For everyone."

"...Including you?"

A beat.

"No."

Just the fact of it, flat, no performance attached. Luna turned her beer in her hands, not drinking, just holding the cold of it.

"My mum used to do this thing...," she started, "— every time I called she'd ask if I was happy like it was a maths problem. Like there was a right answer and I was just taking too long with it."

Aoi blinked. "Were you? Taking too long."

Something flickered in Luna's expression - she made this face, somewhere between caught and amused. "...Probably."

And Aoi's mouth did something. Not quite a smile. Close, though. Closer than usual.

---

The table between them was small. Had always been small. Right now it felt more like a feature than a flaw.

"Are you," Aoi said, after a while.

"...What."

"Happy."

Luna looked at her. Aoi was looking at her beer.

"Right now?"

"In general."

Luna thought about it - actually thought about it, which she didn't always do when people asked. "I don't know... Some days. You?"

Aoi traced the rim of the can with her thumb. A long beat. "I don't think about it that much."

"Because it's easier not to."

"...Yeah."

Luna got up then, took both cans - hers nearly empty, Aoi's still more than half - went to the fridge, got two more. Set one in front of Aoi without asking. Sat back down.

Aoi looked at the new can. Didn't say anything.

Didn't need to.

---

"You know," Luna said, at some point, her chin in her hand, "the other night."

Aoi's hand stilled on the can.

"I'm not— I'm not doing anything about it," Luna added, not looking away. "I just wanted to say. I'm glad you didn't kick me out."

"I kick you out all the time."

"You try. It doesn't stick."

Aoi made a face. The exasperated one, the one Luna knew well, except something underneath it was different tonight - softer, a little. "Which part," she said carefully.

"Both parts."

Aoi looked at the window then. Their reflections again, the table between them, the two cans, the ordinary wreckage of a nothing dinner that had somehow turned into something else.

Luna watched the side of her face.

"...It's fine," Aoi said, eventually. Quiet.

"Which part."

A beat.

"This."

---

Long quiet after that. The earned kind - the kind you had to sit in for a while before you realised you were in it. Luna leaned back in her chair, looked at the ceiling. The overhead light buzzed just faintly. Outside the window the city was doing its late-evening thing, winding down without going away.

"We should get a better light in here," Luna said, to no one particularly.

"We should."

"I'll forget."

"Me too, probably."

Luna smiled, a small one, at the ceiling. Aoi didn't see it.

Or maybe she did, and chose not to comment. Hard to say, with her.

---

They cleaned up in the way they'd gotten used to without deciding to - one rinsed, the other stacked, the leftovers went in the only container that still had a matching lid. No discussion about who did what. The kitchen light off. The hallway dim.

Aoi paused at her bedroom door, hand on the frame.

"...You can stay again," she said. Not looking back. "If you're cold."

Luna just stood there for a second.

"It's always cold."

"I know."

A beat, and then Aoi went in, and after a moment Luna followed, and the light under the door stayed on for a while before it didn't.
